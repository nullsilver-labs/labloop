# FABLE, round 1 — "The Nursery": a throng of ≤10M-parameter organisms whose soul is a curriculum, not weights

Verification key: [V] = checked against a primary or near-primary source today; [S] = secondary source only; [E] = my estimate; [U] = unverified / from memory.

## 1. Core concept

The world is a **nursery of language-organisms**. An *organism* is a ≤10M-parameter model with a *body* (weights, trained fresh every generation, never inherited, per lab rules) and a *genome*: a short text DSL naming (a) the architecture (blocks, widths, attention/SSM/MLP mix, optional non-differentiable modules), (b) the *tokenizer spec* (vocab size, merge rules, digit/byte handling, or a learned-chunk boundary module), (c) the *training recipe* (optimizer, schedule, data mix, compute cap), and (d) a **teaching bundle**: a corpus of ≤N tokens the parent generated and a curriculum order for it. Reproduction = write a child genome (by an agent, by random mutation, or by an organism itself) and train the child body *from scratch* under a fixed compute cap on (real data + the parent's teaching bundle). Selection = hidden-split fitness in **bits-per-byte** (tokenizer-independent, so evolved tokenizers can't cheat) plus a micro-task suite (arithmetic, copy/sort, retrieval, tiny code), scored per component so Labloop's complementary crossover applies. The *environment* has a fixed closed core (the hidden splits) and an open edge: organisms pose tasks to each other. The **Throng** is a set of organisms that must communicate over a discrete 64-symbol channel to solve tasks no single member can; the hive is a research object, not a metaphor. What is inherited across generations is *information that is not weights*: tokenizer, curriculum, language, architecture. That is exactly the Thronglets claim ("the code will rewrite itself") made testable: if non-weight inheritance compounds, recursion is real; if it is model collapse, it isn't.

## 2. The recursive-self-improvement loop, operationalised

Four levels, each with a named improver and a control:

| Level | What improves | Who improves it | Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 weights | body | backprop (default); ES/DUST only for discrete parts | fixed recipe |
| L1 architecture + tokenizer | genome | Claude worker (now); later an *architect organism* (§3.6) | random mutation of the DSL |
| L2 recipe + curriculum | teaching bundle, data mix, schedule | parent organism generates the bundle; worker tunes recipe | real data only, same token count |
| L3 the search itself | job-card text, selection temperature, operator mix | Labloop's existing meta-loop (docs/meta-loop-plan.md) | frozen cards |

**The metric that distinguishes recursion from hill-climbing: transferable lift that grows with generation.** Define `lift_k` = fitness of a *fresh random-init* organism trained with generation-k's inherited artefacts (tokenizer + bundle + architecture) minus fitness of the same organism trained with generation-0 artefacts, at matched compute and seeds. Hill-climbing gives `lift_k` that plateaus or that vanishes when you strip one artefact; recursion gives a positive slope of `lift_k` in k over ≥3 generations, *and* the slope survives training the recipient on a different seed and different real-data shard (so it is not weight or data leakage). Pre-register the slope threshold and the "random-mutation + frozen-card" control arm at the same evaluation count. A second, cheaper recursion signal: the **beneficial-mutation rate** of the L1 improver (fraction of children beating parent) must rise over the campaign; for a frozen operator it is stationary.

## 3. Wow mechanisms (each: mechanism, surprise, claim + kill, cost on 1–2 consumer GPUs, where gradient-free fits)

Cost base [E]: a 10M transformer on one RTX 4090-class GPU trains at a few hundred k tokens/s; a 100M-token run is 10–30 min. "Trainings" below are that unit.

**3.1 Curriculum inheritance (the soul is the syllabus).** Parent generates a teaching bundle (stories it writes, its own best explanations of arithmetic, QA it got right); child trains from scratch on real data + bundle, same total tokens as control. *Surprise:* lineage without weights; a 10M model teaching a 10M model. *Claim:* `lift_3` ≥ +0.02 bits/byte over real-data-only at matched tokens, with the slope positive over k = 1..3. *Kill:* lift ≤ 0 at k = 2 on both seeds — that is Shumailov-style model collapse [U: Nature 2024, from memory] and the premise dies at this scale. *Cost:* 3 gens × 2 arms × 2 seeds = 12 trainings ≈ 4–6 h. *Gradient-free:* none; waste here.

**3.2 Evolved task-specific tokenizer.** Tokenizer spec in the genome (vocab size, merge source corpus, digit splitting, byte fallback, or an H-Net-style learned boundary module [V: arXiv 2507.07955]). Fitness in bits/byte removes the perplexity-vs-vocab confound. *Surprise:* the vocabulary becomes a heritable organ; different species on code vs stories vs numbers. *Claim:* evolved tokenizer gives ≥3% lower BPB on the final split than GPT-2-BPE clipped to the same vocab, with architecture and recipe held fixed (ablation arm). *Kill:* <1%. *Cost:* ~20 trainings. *Gradient-free:* the discrete tokenizer is exactly where evolution belongs; merge tables have no gradient. DUST not needed; population search over specs is enough.

**3.3 Hybrid bodies with non-differentiable organs (ES/DUST, honestly placed).** Organisms whose body contains a discrete module: a hard k-winner-take-all sparse layer, a learned routing table, a symbolic stack scratchpad, or an external calculator call. Differentiable parts: backprop. The discrete interface (≤50k params): EGGROLL-style low-rank ES [V: arXiv 2511.16652, follow-up 2609.10980] or DUST activation perturbation [S: Q Labs; paper reports 4.80 vs 4.63 test loss at 37.7M params/20M tokens; README gives no compute comparison]. *Surprise:* a 10M model doing 8-digit multiplication because it has a non-differentiable organ a pure transformer can't grow. *Claim:* hybrid beats the best pure-transformer candidate on the arithmetic component by ≥20 points at equal param count and ≤3× compute. *Kill:* no better at 3×; or a Gumbel-softmax relaxation matches it (then ES was waste). *Cost:* ES on ≤50k params with population 256 is cheap (a forward pass each, minutes); full-body DUST at 10M is hours–days per training [E] and is **where DUST is a waste at this scale**. Say so in the report.

**3.4 Emergent language, measured.** Speaker organism sees a story, emits ≤8 symbols from a private 64-symbol vocabulary; listener answers hidden questions. Discrete channel, trained with REINFORCE or ES (natural fit; no gradient through symbols). Measures: topographic similarity against shuffled control [V: standard metric, arXiv 2305.12941], and **iterated-learning compression**: a fresh listener learns generation-k's language in fewer episodes than generation-1's (Kirby-style iterated learning [U: from memory]). *Surprise:* the Thronglets melody with a decoder: a probe that maps symbols → story attributes, which the keeper can read. *Claim:* topsim > control by ≥0.15 Spearman and learn-time halves by gen 5. *Kill:* neither by gen 5. *Cost:* small models (≤2M each), ~20 GPU-h total. *Gradient-free:* essential here, cheaply.

**3.5 Throng > member > ensemble.** k organisms, each trained on a disjoint shard of a retrieval task, must answer questions whose evidence is split across shards, communicating via 3.4's channel. *Surprise:* a collective beats the logit-averaged ensemble of the same bodies — division of labour, not voting. *Claim:* throng accuracy ≥ ensemble + 5 points on final. *Kill:* throng ≤ ensemble + noise (2 seeds). Note the pet stays ≤10M; the throng is a population and we say so. *Cost:* ~10 GPU-h. *Gradient-free:* the channel only.

**3.6 Organisms as architects (the organism joins its own evolution).** Train a ≤10M "architect" on the campaign LEDGER (genome DSL → fitness) as surrogate + proposer; let it write child genomes as a Labloop operator beside Claude and random mutation. *Surprise:* a 10M model's proposals accepted more often than random, and rising. *Claim:* beneficial-mutation rate of architect > random by ≥10 points after 200 evaluations and rises across campaign thirds. *Kill:* ≤ random at 200. *Cost:* dominated by evaluations (200 trainings ≈ 3–4 GPU-days); the architect itself is minutes. This is the clean recursion test: L1 improved by an organism from L0. *Gradient-free:* the proposal space is discrete text; the architect is trained by backprop on ledger pairs, selection does the rest.

**3.7 Care as training signal.** Keeper actions are typed events: *feed* = tokens from a chosen domain; *play* = interactive episodes scored by the keeper; *praise/scold* = preference pairs; *neglect* = nothing. Nightly: the pet trains on the day's log with a DPO-like objective [U] plus replay. *Surprise:* two identically-seeded pets diverge measurably by keeper alone. *Claim:* on a held-out personality/skill probe, between-keeper divergence > between-seed divergence by 2×; a keeper who "plays arithmetic" produces a pet ≥10 points better on arithmetic than a story-playing keeper. *Kill:* care effect ≤ seed variance. *Cost:* trivial (minutes/night). Confound to pre-register: care also adds data volume; control with volume-matched random data.

**3.8 Moulting: lifelong growth under a hard cap.** The pet lives in days: wake (tasks), sleep (train on the day), and every N days a **moult**: a child body is trained from scratch on the pet's accumulated teaching bundle + care log; if it beats the pet on hidden probes it replaces it. Identity = curriculum + language + memory, not weights. *Surprise:* a pet that gets smarter for weeks without ever exceeding 10M and without the forgetting curve of naive fine-tuning. *Claim:* after 14 days, day-1 probe drop ≤2 points while day-14 probe gain ≥10 vs. day-1; naive continual fine-tune is the baseline. *Kill:* moulting ≤ "retrain from scratch on everything" at equal compute (also an honest result: then the soul is just the data). *Cost:* ~1 training/day.

**3.9 Open-ended task ecology (POET/OMNI-EPIC-style [V: arXiv 2405.15568]).** Organisms (or the worker) generate tasks from a grammar; a task is kept if "learnable but unsolved" (some organisms pass, not all). *Claim:* population under task co-evolution scores higher on the *fixed closed* final suite than under a fixed curriculum at equal compute (transfer from open to closed). *Kill:* no transfer. *Cost:* ~40 trainings.

**3.10 Species (quality-diversity archive).** MAP-Elites [U: Mouret & Clune 2015, from memory] over descriptors (tokenizer compression ratio, depth/width, arithmetic-vs-story skill) instead of pure rank selection. *Claim:* archive best ≥ rank-selection best at equal evaluations, with ≥4 occupied niches. *Kill:* worse best. Directly addresses Labloop's known bottleneck (draft diversity). *Cost:* same as any campaign.

**3.11 The throng upgrades its keeper (benign ending).** Take the tokenizer and curriculum evolved at ≤10M and apply them to a 50–100M model (outside the cap, one run). *Claim:* ≥2% BPB gain at 50M over default tokenizer/data at matched tokens. *Kill:* no transfer. *Cost:* 2 runs of a few hours.

**3.12 Self-speedrun (harness shakedown).** Fixed 10-minute wall-clock on one GPU, ≤10M params, target BPB; genome = recipe + architecture (nanoGPT-speedrun analogue [S: modded-nanogpt]). *Claim:* time-to-target halves over 30 candidates. Known to work; its value is calibrating matched-compute fitness before anything exotic.

## 4. The pet as functionality

State = genome, body (≤10M), an external memory file (notes the pet writes; not parameters), the care log, the teaching bundle, the private language codebook. Care changes the model through 3.7 nightly; the pet changes the *world* through its bundle and language; the cap is kept through 3.8 moults. Over days it can: answer harder probes (measured), speak a language its lineage shares (3.4), teach its successor (3.1), propose its successor's body (3.6). Nothing here needs a UI; a CLI that logs typed events is the keeper.

## 5. Mapping onto Labloop

- **Campaign** = one pre-registered question (one of §3), `campaign.toml` fixes evaluator, threshold, compute cap. **Candidate** = one organism: `code/` holds genome + training script; `out/predictions-search.json` holds model outputs on search inputs; `config.json` already records seed/commit/hardware. **Baseline c0000** = GPT-2-BPE, 4-layer transformer, AdamW, fixed recipe, same compute cap. **Evaluator** = `score.py` printing BPB + `components_key` per micro-task (complementary crossover already exists for this).
- **Needs new tooling:** (1) **matched-compute enforcement in the evaluator** (FLOP or wall-clock cap, read from the candidate's logged training time; otherwise evolution just trains longer — the main leakage of this whole design); (2) **inheritance channel**: a candidate may read its parent's `out/bundle/` (generated data), with an n-gram dedup check against search inputs logged in `summary.md`; (3) **multi-candidate evaluation** for the throng (evaluator takes a set); (4) a **persistent world store** outside candidate dirs for the pet (care log, memory), read-only to workers; (5) an **organism operator**: `worker_model_by_operator` already lets an operator use a different harness; add one whose "session" is a local ≤10M architect producing a genome, no LLM; (6) `finding.json` cards carrying descriptors so 3.10's archive is a selection mode.
- **No new tooling:** Claude as L1 mutation operator, lineage memory, deferred jobs, hidden labels, REPORT.

## 6. Smallest first experiment, and a roadmap

**Experiment 0 (sub-day): does non-weight inheritance compound or collapse?** TinyStories [V: arXiv 2305.07759] or a 50M-token FineWeb-edu slice; 10M params; 15-minute training cap; fitness = BPB on hidden search split + 3 micro-tasks. Arms: (A) child trains on real data + parent's teaching bundle (30% of tokens), 3 generations; (B) real data only, same tokens; 2 seeds each; c0000 the baseline. Pre-fixed: `lift_2 ≥ +0.01 BPB` on the final split counts as "inheritance works"; `lift_2 ≤ 0` on both seeds kills the curriculum axis and the project pivots to tokenizer/architecture evolution with Claude as the only improver. 12–14 trainings ≈ 5 h on one GPU; checks written down: bundle deduped against search inputs, child weights initialised from seed only (hash the init).

**Stage 1 (weeks 1–2):** 3.12 shakedown + 3.2 tokenizer + 3.10 archive: one campaign each, ~30 candidates. **Stage 2 (weeks 3–5):** 3.4 language → 3.5 throng; 3.7 care + 3.8 moult on one living pet. **Stage 3 (weeks 6–8):** 3.6 architect operator vs Claude vs random (the real recursion test), 3.3 hybrid bodies with ES, 3.11 upward transfer.

## 7. Biggest risks and doubts

1. **Model collapse is the default outcome** of 3.1 at 10M quality; the whole "soul is the curriculum" story may die on day one. Good: that is what Experiment 0 is for.
2. **Throng = ensemble.** Most "collective intelligence" results at small scale are voting in disguise; the ensemble control must be strong (weighted, calibrated), or 3.5 is theatre.
3. **DUST is hype at this scale.** Everything a 10M model needs is differentiable; gradient-free pays only for tokenizers, channels and small discrete organs. I expect 3.3's kill criterion (Gumbel relaxation matches ES) to fire. We should say in advance that this is a plausible outcome.
4. **Compute-cap leakage** is the attack surface: longer training, bigger effective batch, eval-time compute. If the evaluator doesn't enforce the cap, every result is void.
5. **Claude-as-mutation-operator confound.** Gains in L1 may be Claude's prior knowledge, not the world's recursion. Only 3.6 (architect from the ledger, no LLM) and the frozen-random control separate them; everything before Stage 3 is "agent-driven NAS", which is useful but not Thronglets.
6. **The individual pet will be dumb.** A 10M model will not wow anyone in conversation; the wow has to come from measured trajectories, lineage effects and the collective. If the user wants a conversational wow, this design will disappoint.
7. **Topographic similarity is fragile** [V: alignment confounds, arXiv 2407.17960]; the emergent-language claim needs the iterated-learning measure as well, or it is a number without a story.
8. **Evaluation cost dominates**: every mutation is a training run. 200 evaluations for 3.6 is days of GPU; the usage window and GPU hours must both be budgeted, and Labloop's deferral already handles the former.
